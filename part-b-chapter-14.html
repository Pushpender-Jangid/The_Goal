01. Introduction to Workplace Quality Measures

Chapter Focus

Workplace Quality Measures focuses on maintaining a healthy, clean, comfortable and safe working environment.

The chapter particularly discusses air quality, water quality, workplace cleanliness, office ergonomics and safe computer usage.

📌 Why Workplace Quality Matters

  • Good air quality improves the working capability of employees.
  • Pollution-free air is an important requirement for an organisation.
  • Clean water helps prevent diseases caused by polluted water.
  • A clean workplace reduces hazards and makes work easier and more pleasant.
  • Proper ergonomics can reduce accidents and injuries and improve performance and productivity.

02. Air and Water Quality Monitoring

Air Quality

Organisations should maintain good air quality because polluted air can negatively affect employees and the surrounding environment.

Water Quality

Water pollution is another important concern. Polluted water may cause diseases, therefore an organisation should take measures to prevent water pollution.

Three Forms of Analysis

Air and water pollution can be analysed using three common forms of analysis:

  1. Physical analysis
  2. Chemical analysis
  3. Biological analysis
🧠 EXAM MEMORY

Physical + Chemical + Biological

= Three common forms of analysis

Physical Analysis

Physical properties such as temperature and the content of a sample can be measured.

Chemical Analysis

Chemical analysis can measure substances and properties such as the pH value of water.

Biological Analysis

The effect of air and water on plants and animals can be studied. Microbial indicators are used to monitor the health of the ecosystem.

03. Guidelines for Clean Air and Clean Water

🌱 Must Remember

  1. Air pollution is mostly caused by dust, solid particles and gases in surrounding air.
  2. Organisations should avoid unnecessary production of dust, solid particles and gases.
  3. Excessive use of automobiles in the campus can contribute to air pollution.
  4. The organisation should use a limited number of vehicles and practise a no-vehicle day every week.
  5. Ozone produced in air can pollute the air and is associated with smog.
  6. The generation of ozone gas should be kept at a low level.
  7. Sewage and waste water can cause water pollution.
  8. Waste should not be allowed to mix with surrounding water.
  9. Extensive use of fertilizers and pesticides should be avoided because they can pollute ground water.
✏️ EXAM POINT

Remember: Dust → Vehicles → Ozone → Sewage → Fertilizers/Pesticides

04. Importance of Cleanliness at Workplace

Keeping the workplace clean is important for preventing hazardous situations.

Poor handling and storage practices can result in damages. A clean workstation makes work easier and more pleasant.

🧹 Clean Workplace Rules

  • All areas should be kept neat and clean.
  • Each employee is responsible for the cleanliness of their work area and tools/equipment.
  • Spills and breakage should be cleaned immediately.
  • Spaces around machines and equipment should remain clear and clean for free movement.
  • Floors should be kept clean and clear to prevent slipping and collision.
  • Lighting fixtures should be checked regularly to provide clear vision.
  • Faulty lights should be reported to the administration.

05. Office Ergonomics

What is Ergonomics?

Ergonomics is the science concerned with designing and arranging things so that people can use them easily and safely.

Applying ergonomics can reduce the potential for accidents and injuries and can improve performance and productivity.

Early Warning Sign Potential Cause What to Do
Sore lower back No lumbar support Use chair backrest, small pillow or lumbar support.
Burning in upper back No upper-back support Use a document holder or raise the document.
Stiff neck Head turned to side / tilted forward Move or raise monitor to the centre of the desk.
Sore shoulders Reaching forward for long periods Move closer to keyboard and mouse.
Arching wrists Wrists extended too much with repetition Use suitable wrist support and maintain proper position.
Dry eyes Forgetting to blink Rest eyes periodically and perform simple eye exercises.
Eye strain / sore eyes Glare from lights or windows Re-orient desk and computer to reduce direct glare.
🧠 EXAM MEMORY

Ergonomics = Design + Arrangement + Easy & Safe Use

06. Computer Health and Safety Tips

Increased use of computers can lead to health and safety issues related to vision, musculoskeletal problems, body aches and pains.

Many such problems are preventable and can be addressed through simple corrective actions.

Musculoskeletal Problems

Musculoskeletal problems can affect areas such as the neck, back, chest, arms, shoulders and feet .

They may occur because of wrong posture or an uncomfortable chair that is not ergonomically suitable.

📌 Correct Computer Posture

  • Keep the end of the monitor at eye level.
  • Keep the neck neutral and the monitor directly ahead.
  • Keep the monitor approximately an arm's length away, or about 20–30 inches.
  • Maximise contact of the back against the chair backrest.
  • Keep elbows at an approximately 100–110° open angle.
  • Keep the keyboard at a slight negative tilt when sitting upright.
  • Hands should be slightly lower than elbows while typing.
  • Minimise twisting of wrists.
  • Do not rest wrists on the palm rest while actually typing.
  • Take small breaks to stretch muscles and rest the eyes.

07. Occupational Overuse Syndrome

Definition

Occupational Overuse Syndrome is also known as Repetition Strain Injury (RSI) .

It is a collective term for conditions involving discomfort or persistent pain in muscles, tendons and other soft tissues.

Main Causes

  • Repetitive movement
  • Sustained or constrained postures
  • Forceful movements
  • Psycho-social factors such as workplace stress

Carpal Tunnel Syndrome

Repetitive use of muscles can cause pain in the neck, shoulder, wrist or fingers.

Carpal Tunnel Syndrome is one of the common conditions related to repetitive computer use.

It may cause pain, numbness and tingling in the hand and arm.

🧠 EXAM MEMORY

Occupational Overuse Syndrome = Repetition Strain Injury (RSI)

Repetition + Wrong posture + Forceful movement → Risk of overuse problems

Keyboard and Mouse Position

  • Keep fingers above the home position: ASDF and JKL;.
  • Keep elbows by the sides.
  • Avoid gripping the mouse too tightly.
  • Keep keyboard and mouse at the same level.
  • Ergonomic keyboard and mouse can reduce wrist-related risks.

08. Strain in Legs and Feet

Sitting for a long time while working can cause strain in the legs.

🪑 Correct Sitting Position

  • Sit comfortably in the desk chair.
  • Keep feet flat on the floor.
  • Keep lower legs vertical.
  • Use a footrest when additional support is needed.
  • Keep enough space to change position.
  • Stretch legs from time to time.

09. Eye Strain and Computer Vision Syndrome

Bright light, glare and flickering images from a computer can cause eye strain and visual fatigue .

Constantly focusing on the screen can reduce blinking, which may contribute to dry eyes.

Computer Vision Syndrome

Computer Vision Syndrome is associated with poor lighting and glare on the computer screen.

Possible symptoms include blurry vision, burning or watering eyes, headaches and sometimes shoulder and neck pain.

👁️ How to Reduce Visual Problems

  • Adjust the brightness of the computer screen.
  • Reposition the screen to avoid glare.
  • Maintain proper viewing distance.
  • Blink your eyes regularly.
  • Use anti-glare glasses where appropriate.
  • Keep the screen clean.
  • Use a desk lamp when required.
  • Ensure screen colours and characters are easy to see.
  • Take periodic breaks from the screen.
  • Look away into the distance to relax the eyes.
  • Keep the monitor around 18–24 inches away from the face.
✏️ VERY IMPORTANT

The textbook suggests looking at something about 30 metres away for 30 seconds every 30 minutes to relax the eyes.

10. Headaches, Obesity and Stress Disorders

Headaches

Headaches may occur due to muscle tension or neck pain. Eye strain and vision problems can also cause headaches.

Regular eye examinations, maintaining a straight neck position and taking breaks can help address such problems.

Obesity

Spending long hours on computers can reduce physical activity and exercise.

Prolonged use of computers or electronic devices can contribute to obesity.

Taking breaks and including physical activity is recommended.

Stress Disorders

Prolonged computer use and increased workplace pressure may contribute to stress.

Untreated stress may contribute to more serious health problems and can lead to decreased attention span, lack of concentration, dizziness and burnout.

🧠 EXAM MEMORY

Long computer use can be associated with:

Eye problems + Body problems + Reduced activity + Stress

11. Injuries from Laptop Use

Laptops are convenient but their screen and keyboard are very close together.

This creates an ergonomic difficulty because positioning the screen correctly for the back and neck may make the keyboard uncomfortable for the arms and shoulders, and vice versa.

💻 Exam Point

The textbook explains that there is no single right way to use a laptop for long periods because the screen and keyboard are attached.

For long working hours, an ergonomically arranged desktop setup may be preferable.

12. Sleeping Problems

Artificial lighting from computer screens can affect the body's release of melatonin, a substance associated with sleeping patterns.

To tackle this problem, the textbook advises avoiding computer use immediately before going to bed.

✏️ EXAM POINT

Computer screen light → Melatonin suppression → Possible sleeping problems.

13. Health and Safety Requirements for Computer Workplace

The chapter gives minimum health and safety requirements for desktop computers, laptops, tablets, smartphones, television screens and video monitors.

Area Important Requirement
Display Screen Use a suitable modern LED monitor with legible characters, adequate spacing, stable image and no distracting glare/reflections.
Keyboard Keyboard should allow a comfortable working position, have sufficient support space and a matt surface to reduce glare.
Work Surface Desk should be sufficiently large, low-reflectance and allow flexible arrangement of equipment and documents.
Document Holder Should be stable and adjustable to reduce uncomfortable head and eye movements.
Work Chair Stable, movable and height-adjustable. Feet should rest flat on the floor or on a footrest.
Space Enough space should be available to change position and vary movements.
Lighting Should provide satisfactory conditions and suitable contrast while avoiding disturbing glare and reflections.
Noise & Heat Equipment noise should not distract attention and equipment should not produce excessive heat.

14. Reflections, Glare, Noise and Heat

Reflections and Glare

Workstations should be designed so that windows, openings, transparent or translucent walls and bright fixtures do not create direct glare or distracting reflections.

Windows should have suitable adjustable coverings to reduce excessive daylight falling on the workstation.

Noise

Noise emitted by equipment should not distract the user's attention.

Noise-cancelling earphones may help when some noise cannot be avoided.

Heat

Equipment should not produce excessive heat that causes discomfort to users.

15. Cautions While Working on the Computer

⚠️ Must Remember

  1. Use a surge protector when plugging the computer system into power.
  2. A battery backup system helps protect against power outages by providing constant voltage.
  3. Remove rings, watches and necklaces while working on computer because conductive metals may damage computer components through static electricity.
  4. Unplug power sources and cables before working inside or on computer hardware.
  5. Loose cords and wires can create hazards and electrical hazards.
  6. If a cord crosses a pathway, mark it with hazard tape.
  7. Avoid water while working with electricity.
  8. Never touch or repair electrical equipment or circuits with wet hands.
