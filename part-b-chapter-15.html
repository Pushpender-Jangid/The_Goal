01. Introduction to Accidents and Emergencies

Chapter Focus

In an organisation, a small accident or unforeseen situation may turn into an emergency if it does not receive proper attention. Emergencies may be natural or man-made and can disrupt operations or cause physical and environmental damage.

Natural Emergencies

  • Floods
  • Hurricanes
  • Earthquakes

Man-made Emergencies

  • Fire
  • Toxic gas releases
  • Chemical spills
  • Illness
  • Explosions
  • Civil disturbances

02. Accident and Emergency
Accident Emergency
An accident is an unplanned, uncontrolled or unforeseen event resulting in injury or harm to people and damage to goods. An emergency is a serious or crisis situation that needs immediate attention and action.
Example: A person falling down and getting injured. Example: A customer having a heart attack or sudden outbreak of fire.
✏️ EXAM POINT

Emergency → serious or crisis situation requiring immediate attention and action.

03. Guidelines for Accidents and Emergencies

1. Notice and Correctly Identify

Employees should know what constitutes an accident and what constitutes an emergency. The organisation's policies and guidelines are the best guide for identifying and handling such situations.

2. Get Help Promptly

Follow the procedure for handling the particular accident or emergency and provide the required help and support according to organisational policies. Do not act outside the guidelines of your role.

3. Prevent Further Injury

If someone is injured, do not act according to impulse or gut feeling. Stay calm and follow the prescribed organisational procedure while waiting for help.

4. Act Within Responsibility and Authority

Provide help and support only within your authorised limits. Medical help should be provided by a person who is certified to provide the necessary aid.

5. Follow Instructions

Follow instructions given by senior staff and emergency services. When handling accidents or emergencies, work under the supervision of trained personnel.

🧠 Remember the sequence:

Identify → Get Help → Prevent Further Injury → Act Within Authority → Follow Instructions

04. Types of Accidents

1. Trip and Fall

A person may trip on carelessly left loose material such as loose wires, goods left in aisles or an elevated threshold. Such accidents may result in bruises or serious fractures.

2. Slip and Fall

Slippery surfaces or substances on the floor can cause a person to lose balance and fall.

3. Injuries Caused by Escalators or Elevators

Accidents may occur while using escalators, elevators or lifts. Proper care and organisational procedures should be followed.

4. Accidents Due to Falling Goods

Goods or materials that are not properly stored may fall and cause injury to people or damage to property.

5. Accidents Due to Moving Objects

Moving objects or equipment can cause accidents when people come into their path or when safe operating procedures are not followed.

✏️ EXAM POINT

  4. Accidents due to Falling of Goods

Attend to the Injured Person Immediately

The injured person should receive immediate attention according to the organisation's prescribed procedure and within the helper's authorised limits.

Inform Your Supervisor

The supervisor should be informed promptly about the accident.

Assist Your Supervisor

Provide necessary assistance to the supervisor while following the organisation's procedures and your authorised responsibilities.

⚠️ Important

Do not provide medical assistance beyond your qualification or authorisation. Properly trained and certified professionals should handle situations requiring specialised decisions.

07. General Evacuation Procedures

What is Evacuation?

Evacuation means moving people away from a dangerous situation or workplace area to a safe location.

Emergency Evacuation Planning

An organisation should have an emergency action plan that guides employees during emergencies. Employees should be aware of the evacuation procedures applicable to their workplace.

Evacuation Routes and Exits

  • Know the available emergency exits.
  • Be aware of evacuation routes.
  • Follow the organisation's evacuation instructions.
  • Do not obstruct emergency exits or routes.
  • Follow instructions of trained staff and emergency services.

🚪 Remember

During an emergency, follow the established evacuation procedure instead of acting according to personal impulse.

08. Fire Hazards in the Workplace

Fire Hazard

Fire can become a major workplace emergency and can cause injury, property damage and disruption of operations.

Fire Prevention

Fire prevention requires identification and control of potential fire hazards. Employees should know the organisation's fire safety procedures and emergency arrangements.

Material and Ignition Sources

Fire requires combustible material and a source capable of igniting it. The textbook therefore emphasises identifying material and ignition sources when preventing workplace fires.

✏️ EXAM POINT

Workplace fires are commonly associated with improper storage of combustible material and combustible dust.

Fire Extinguisher

A fire extinguisher is a protection device containing an extinguishing agent that can be discharged to extinguish a fire.

🧠 Fire Safety Recall
  • Identify fire hazards.
  • Identify combustible materials.
  • Identify ignition sources.
  • Follow workplace fire procedures.
  • Use fire protection equipment only as trained.

09. First Aid for Emergencies

First Aid

First aid refers to immediate care provided to an injured or ill person before professional medical assistance is available, within the limits of the person's training and authority.

Important Principle

If you are not certified or trained to provide a particular medical aid, do not attempt procedures beyond your authorisation. Wait for the appropriate professionals and provide assistance as instructed.

⚠️ Safety Rule

Stay calm → follow organisational procedure → get appropriate help → do not exceed your authority.

10. Electrical Safety and Emergencies

Electrical Emergency

Electrical emergencies require careful action because contact with electricity can cause serious injury. The organisation's electrical safety procedures must be followed.

Basic Safety Principles

  • Follow the organisation's electrical safety procedure.
  • Do not take unauthorised action.
  • Switch off electrical circuits when instructed and when it is safe and authorised to do so.
  • Keep away from electrical hazards.
  • Seek help from trained personnel.

First Aid for Electrical Emergencies

The textbook includes first aid for electrical emergencies as an important part of this chapter. If a person is affected by an electrical emergency, the appropriate electrical rescue procedure and first-aid procedure should be followed.

11. Electrical Rescue Techniques

Why Electrical Rescue Requires Care

An electrical emergency must be handled carefully to prevent additional injury. The first priority is to follow the prescribed organisational procedure and obtain appropriate assistance.

⚠️ Exam Safety Concept

A person should not take actions beyond their training, certification or authority during an electrical emergency. Trained professionals should handle specialised rescue and medical procedures.

12. Emergency Action Plan

Why is an Emergency Action Plan Needed?

An emergency action plan provides employees with a planned course of action during an emergency. It helps an organisation respond systematically instead of depending on confusion or personal impulse.

Key Areas to Remember

  1. Identify possible emergencies.
  2. Know organisational policies and procedures.
  3. Know how to report accidents and emergencies.
  4. Know emergency exits and evacuation routes.
  5. Know whom to contact for help.
  6. Follow instructions of trained personnel and emergency services.
  7. Act only within your responsibility and authority.
