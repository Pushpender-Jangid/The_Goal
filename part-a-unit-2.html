SESSION 01

Stress Management

EXAM IMPORTANT

What is Stress?

Stress is our emotional, mental, physical and social reaction to perceived demands or threats.

Stressors are the demands or threats that cause stress.

Examples of Stressors

  • Feeling unprepared when examinations are near.
  • Loss of someone close in the family.
  • Worrying about what other people think.
  • Lack of sleep.

Stress Management

Stress management means making a plan to cope effectively with daily pressures and maintaining a balance between work, relationships, relaxation and fun.

★ Remember:
Stress may sometimes motivate us, but excessive or prolonged stress can negatively affect our emotional and physical health and our ability to function effectively.

ABC of Stress Management

Letter Meaning Exam Point
A Adversity The stressful event or situation.
B Beliefs The way we respond to the event.
C Consequences Actions and outcomes resulting from the response.

Three Steps to Manage Stress

Step What to do?
1. Be aware Recognise signs of stress such as headache, sleeplessness, sadness, excessive worrying and nervousness.
2. Identify the cause Find out what is causing the stress.
3. Apply management methods Use suitable methods such as time management, talking to someone, exercise and relaxation.

Stress Management Techniques

  • Time management — plan and use time properly.
  • Physical exercise and fresh air — maintain a healthy routine.
  • Yoga, meditation and deep breathing — help the body relax.
  • Relaxation activities — take time to relax.
  • Talk to someone close when a problem is difficult to handle alone.

Quick Recall

Stress → Stressor → ABC → 3 Steps → Management Techniques

SESSION 02

Self-awareness — Strength and Weakness Analysis

EXAM IMPORTANT

Meaning of Self-awareness

Self-awareness means being able to identify and understand your own strengths and weaknesses.

It involves understanding who you are, your likes and dislikes, values, beliefs, opinions, background, abilities and areas where you need improvement.

★ Main idea: Self-awareness helps us know what we can and cannot do, plan how to improve, and become more confident.

Strengths

Strengths are abilities or qualities in which a person performs well.

Examples:

  • Good at creative writing.
  • Confident while speaking before an audience.
  • Good at playing a musical instrument.

Weaknesses

Weaknesses are areas where a person struggles or needs improvement.

Examples:

  • Difficulty in solving certain mathematics problems.
  • Wanting to improve English speaking skills.
  • Difficulty accepting loss in games or sports.

How to Identify Strengths?

  • Think about things you are always successful at.
  • Think about what others like or appreciate in you.
  • Take time to think about what you do well.

How to Identify Weaknesses?

  • Identify areas where you struggle.
  • Consider the feedback others usually give you.
  • Accept weaknesses without feeling low.
  • Treat weaknesses as areas for improvement.
★ Important:
A weakness should not simply be considered a negative quality. It can be treated as an area that needs improvement.

Questions for Strength & Weakness Analysis

  • How am I different from others?
  • What do I do better than others?
  • What do other people admire in me?
  • What makes me stand out?
  • Where do I struggle?
  • What advice for improvement do I receive from others?

Interests vs Abilities

Interests Abilities
Things that you like to do in your free time. An acquired or natural capacity.
Things you are curious about or want to learn. Enable you to perform a task with considerable proficiency.
Things you enjoy or would like to do in the future. Skills or capacities that help you perform a particular job or task.
Example:
A student may be interested in music but may not currently have the ability to sing well. The student can either improve the required ability or consider another suitable path.

Quick Recall

Self-awareness = Knowing yourself + Strengths + Weaknesses + Interests + Abilities

SESSION 03

Self-motivation

VERY IMPORTANT

Meaning of Self-motivation

Self-motivation is the force within us that drives us to do things.

It is the ability to do the things that need to be done without someone or something influencing us.

Exam definition:
Self-motivation is the ability to motivate oneself and continue working towards goals without depending on external influence.

Types of Motivation

Type Meaning Example
Internal Motivation We do something because it makes us happy, healthy or feel good. Learning a new skill because you enjoy learning it.
External Motivation We do something because it gives us respect, recognition, appreciation or a reward. Practising hard to win a prize in a competition.
★ Memory Trick:
Internal = LOVE
External = REWARD

Qualities of Self-motivated People

  • Know what they want from life.
  • Know what is important to them.
  • Are focused.
  • Are dedicated to fulfilling their dreams.

Building Self-motivation — Four Steps

Step What it means
1. Find out your strengths Identify your likes, dislikes and strengths.
2. Set and focus on your goals Define the goals you want to achieve and focus your energy on them.
3. Develop a plan Set timelines and list activities needed to achieve each goal.
4. Stay loyal to your goals Continue working towards the goal even during difficult times.

Quick Recall

Strengths → Goals → Plan → Stay Loyal

SESSION 04

Self-regulation — Goal Setting

HIGH SCORING TOPIC

What is a Goal?

A goal is a set of dreams with a deadline to achieve them.

Example: Saving pocket money to buy something by a particular date.

What is Goal Setting?

Goal setting is the process of finding and listing your goals and planning how to achieve them.

★ Why are goals important?
Goals help us separate important work from less important work and focus on the final result.

SMART Goals

Letter Meaning Exam Understanding
S Specific The goal should be clear and specific.
M Measurable There should be a way to measure progress or achievement.
A Achievable The goal should be possible to accomplish.
R Realistic The goal should be practical and appropriate.
T Time-bound The goal should have a definite time limit or deadline.
★ Most Important MCQ:
SMART = Specific + Measurable + Achievable + Realistic + Time-bound

Example of a Time-bound Goal

“I want to improve my performance in the next six months.”

A goal without a time limit is not time-bound.

Short-term and Long-term Goals

Short-term Goals Long-term Goals
Goals planned for a comparatively shorter period, such as the next few months or up to a couple of years. Goals planned for a longer period, such as several years.
Exam Tip:
While setting a goal, make sure it is clear, measurable, possible, practical and has a deadline.

Quick Recall

Goal → Plan → SMART → Short-term / Long-term

SESSION 05

Self-regulation — Time Management

VERY IMPORTANT

What is Time Management?

Time management is the ability to plan and control how you spend the hours of your day so that you can complete the things you want to do effectively.

Time Management Helps Us To:

  • Complete tasks on time.
  • Prepare and follow a daily timetable.
  • Estimate how long a task may take.
  • Submit homework and assignments on time.
  • Avoid wasting time.
★ Exam Point:
Good time management includes planning, prioritising, controlling distractions and tracking how time is spent.

Four Steps for Effective Time Management

Step Meaning
1. Organise Plan day-to-day activities, make a timetable, keep surroundings and study table organised, and put things back where they belong.
2. Prioritise Make a to-do list and rank activities according to their importance. Complete important tasks first.
3. Control Control activities and time. Avoid time-wasters and focus on important work.
4. Track Note where you spend your time. Analyse whether your time was used effectively and identify time-wasting activities.
★ Four-step sequence:

ORGANISE → PRIORITISE → CONTROL → TRACK

Tips for Effective Time Management

  • Avoid delaying or postponing planned activities.
  • Keep your room and study desk organised.
  • Create a “No Disturbance Zone” for important work.
  • Use waiting time productively.
  • Prepare a to-do list.
  • Prioritise important tasks.
  • Replace useless activities with productive activities.
Relatable Example:
If you have homework, revision and entertainment to manage, first list the tasks, give priority to important study work, control distractions such as unnecessary phone use, and later check where your time was actually spent.

Quick Recall

Organise → Prioritise → Control → Track
