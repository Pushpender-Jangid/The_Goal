01.Introduction to Health, Safety and Security

Chapter Focus

Health, Safety and Security at Workplace focuses on creating a safe workplace, protecting employees, identifying hazards and applying control measures.

Health

The textbook defines employee health as physical, mental and social well-being. Workplace cleanliness, proper air conditioning, filtered water, fresh food and clean washrooms are highlighted.

Safety

The workplace should be free from hazards and risk. A hazard can cause harm, while risk is the probability of causing harm. Safety guidelines should be prepared, followed and practised regularly.

Security

Security means freedom from potential harm. The security department is responsible for areas such as personal safety, computer system safety, electrical safety, transport safety and equipment safety.

02.Policies and Procedures

Policies and Procedures

A health, safety and security policy is a written statement of an employer's commitment. Organisations should identify hazards and specify control measures.

Reasons for Policies

Policies show commitment to employee health and safety, outline accountability, support compliance with Occupational Health and Safety policy, and help prevent injuries and illness.

— ✦ — ✦ — ✦ —
03.Workplace Safety Hazards

Workplace Safety Hazards

The chapter discusses biological, chemical, physical, ergonomic and safety hazards. Physical hazards can arise from the physical work environment.

Physical Hazards

Falls, slips, unsafe surfaces, machinery and electricity can create physical hazards. Spills should be cleaned immediately, lighting should be adequate and damaged equipment should be repaired.

Electrical Hazards

Electrical accidents can involve live wires, conductors, exposed or worn-out wiring, overloaded outlets, ungrounded/faulty equipment and unsafe use. Keep water and food away from electrical equipment.

Fire Hazards

Fire safety requires proper housekeeping, emergency systems and awareness of exits, escape routes, fire extinguishers and alarms.

— ✦ — ✦ — ✦ —
04.Health & Computer Workplace Hazards

Health Hazards

Workplace hazards can affect body parts and systems. Noise may affect hearing; bright lights and toxic fumes/vapours may affect eyes and nose; harmful chemicals may affect lungs.

Computer Hazards

Poor sitting posture, prolonged sitting, repetitive movements and screen glare can cause pain, strain, muscle fatigue and eye problems. Regular stretching can help mitigate these hazards.

Office Equipment

Improper handling of office equipment can cause injuries such as cuts from sharp-edged equipment. Proper training and relevant manuals are recommended.

— ✦ — ✦ — ✦ —
05.Stress, Working Environment & Hazard Control

Handling Objects

Heavy objects should be lifted or moved using approved procedures and proper posture.

Stress at Work

Long working hours and aggressive conflicts or arguments can create stress. Conflict resolution and relaxing hobbies are suggested in the textbook.

Working Environment

Poor ventilation, inappropriate chair/table height, hard furniture, poor lighting, lack of emergency awareness and poor housekeeping can be hazards. Physical or emotional intimidation is also mentioned.

Hazard Control

Control measures follow a hierarchy: eliminate the hazard where possible; if not, isolate it; if neither is possible, minimise the likelihood of injury using effective controls such as exhaust ventilation and PPE.

— ✦ — ✦ — ✦ —
06.Safety Guidelines Checklist

📌 Must Remember

  1. Store cleaning chemicals in tightly closed containers in separate cupboards.
  2. Throw garbage daily and make sure areas have proper lighting.
  3. Do not wear loose clothing or jewellery while working with machines.
  4. Where required, wear protective items such as goggles, safety glasses, masks, gloves and hair nets.
  5. Shut down machines before leaving.
  6. Do not play with electrical controls or switches.
  7. Do not operate machines/equipment until properly trained and allowed by the supervisor.
  8. Repair torn wires or broken plugs before use.
  9. Do not use equipment if it smokes, sparks or looks unsafe.
  10. Cover food, follow No Smoking areas and report unsafe conditions or acts to the supervisor.
